Power flow and voltage stability

Power systems lab (ECE 4464) · With a lab partner · 2025

A PowerWorld study of how a simple transmission system responds as one generator pushes more and more power into it.

Setup

The model has three buses: the generator on a 22 kV bus (Bus 1), a 400 kV mid-line bus (Bus 2), and a 400 kV infinite bus (Bus 3) that holds 1.0 p.u. at 0°. We raised the generator’s output from 100 to 700 MW in 100 MW steps, with its terminal voltage held at 1.0 p.u., and recorded voltages, angles, reactive power, and losses at each step.

Angles are relative to the infinite bus at 0°. Negative generator Q means the generator is absorbing reactive power. Losses are generator output minus the power delivered to the infinite bus.

Up to 300 MW the generator is absorbing reactive power and the mid-line voltage sits above 1.0 p.u. From there the voltage drops faster with each step: 1.012, 1.001, 0.984, then 0.962 p.u. at 700 MW. To hold its own terminal voltage, the generator goes from absorbing 50 Mvar to supplying 208 Mvar. Losses grow with the square of the current, from under 1 MW to about 33 MW.

The generator angle reached 46.9° at 700 MW. The case still solved, so 700 MW is not the stability limit, but the falling voltage and rising reactive demand show the system moving toward it.

Reducing losses and supporting voltage

In the report we looked at ways to improve this: shunt capacitors or a STATCOM at the mid-line bus to supply reactive power locally, larger or bundled conductors to cut resistance and reactance, and a higher transmission voltage to reduce the current for the same power.
